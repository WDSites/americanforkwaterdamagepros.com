AC Leak Water Damage Repair in American Fork, UT

A leaking air conditioner usually shows up as a stained ceiling or a wet furnace closet. We find the wet material, open it up and dry it before mold has a damp place to start.

Call (385) 988-9410

When an air handler sits in an attic or a furnace closet, its leaks do not stay put. Condensate drips into a pan, the pan overflows, and the water soaks insulation and the drywall ceiling below. In American Fork the first sign is often a brown ring on a hallway or bedroom ceiling.

Plumbing, water heater and appliance leaks make up a bigger share of water losses than AC leaks, but summer cooling does produce them. For the more common case, a failed supply line, see our burst pipe water damage page.

Why air conditioners leak

NOAA's 1991 to 2020 climate normals put the July average high at 95.2 F at Provo BYU, the nearest normals station to American Fork. The figure is for Provo, not American Fork itself, but it shows how hard cooling systems work in July.

A cooling system pulls moisture out of the air as it chills it. That water, called condensate, drips off the evaporator coil into a pan and leaves the house through a drain line. If the line clogs, the pan fills and overflows.

A frozen evaporator coil can cause trouble too. When the ice thaws, it can release more water than the pan was built to carry. Finding out why the coil froze is a job for an HVAC technician.

The Department of Energy's air conditioner guide mentions plugged condensate drains as a mold concern, which is one reason not to leave wet insulation in place.

Evaporative coolers are a different problem

The Department of Energy says evaporative coolers suit low-humidity climates and need a major cleaning each season. A leaking evaporative cooler is a supply-water problem, since it runs on a water line and a pan. We do not service the cooler, and we dry whatever it wet.

Safety steps in the first hour

  1. Turn off the AC

    Switch the thermostat to off, then turn off the air handler's breaker. A running unit keeps making condensate.

  2. Protect the room below

    Move furniture and set out buckets. If the ceiling is bulging with water, keep people out from under it and call dispatch so the water can be released in a controlled way.

  3. Watch for electrical hazards

    Do not touch fixtures or outlets near a wet ceiling. Turn off that circuit at the panel if lights flicker.

  4. Do not enter a wet attic

    Wet insulation hides joists and wiring. Leave the attic alone until it is dry.

  5. Call both trades

    Call an HVAC technician for the unit and call us for the water.

How we dry out an AC leak

  • Locate the wet ceiling and insulation with a thermal camera and moisture meters.
  • Remove soaked insulation and open the drywall where needed.
  • Place air movers and dehumidifiers, and recheck readings until the cavity reads dry.
  • Clean affected surfaces and remove materials that cannot be dried.

Water from a clogged condensate line is usually Category 1 or 2, depending on what the pan collected. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the reference for sorting categories. Drywall and paint repair afterward is scoped separately on water damage repair.

Insurance and mistakes to avoid

Policies differ, so ask your insurer how they treat an AC leak. A sudden overflow and a long slow drip are often handled differently. Take photos, keep records and avoid permanent repairs before the insurer inspects.

  • Running the AC while the pan is overflowing.
  • Covering a stained ceiling with paint before it is dry.
  • Leaving wet insulation up there for weeks.
