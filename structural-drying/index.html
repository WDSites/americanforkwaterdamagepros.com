Structural Drying and Dehumidification in American Fork, UT

Getting standing water out is only the start. Structural drying is the measured work of pulling moisture out of framing, subfloor, drywall and concrete, then proving with readings that it is gone.

Call (385) 988-9410

After a pipe break or roof leak in American Fork, the wall can look normal while the insulation, bottom plate and drywall paper stay wet inside. NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals for Salt Lake City, the nearest long-record station and not American Fork itself, show 15.52 inches of precipitation a year. That is useful context, but a sealed wall cavity cannot trade moisture with outdoor air, so dry weather alone does not dry it.

This page covers the science and the monitoring. The first hours of a loss are on our water mitigation page.

How wet buildings dry

Water leaves a material by turning to vapor, and the speed depends on three things: temperature, airflow across the surface and the difference in vapor pressure between the wet material and the room air. Equipment is chosen to push on those three levers.

Every material also has its own pace. Drywall and carpet give up water quickly. Framing, plaster, hardwood and concrete hold it much longer. That is why the EPA says wet areas should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, yet its flood cleanup page notes that a full structure can take several days to weeks.

What you see that points to wet structure

  • Baseboards that cup or pull away from the wall.
  • A cool, damp feel on a wall or floor with no visible water.
  • Humidity that stays high even with the heat on.

Surface dry is not dry

A dry finish says little about the stud bay behind it. Readings from unaffected walls in your own home set the target, and wet areas stay on equipment until they match.

Safety and first-hour steps before drying starts

  1. Stop the source

    Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff. Drying cannot start until the water stops.

  2. Keep electrical away from wet walls

    Turn off breakers for wet rooms and outlets. Do not plug in a household fan or extension cord on a wet floor.

  3. Open doors and drawers

    Move furniture off wet floors and open cabinet doors so air can reach the wall.

  4. Keep the heat at a normal setting

    A regular indoor temperature helps materials release moisture. Do not turn the furnace off to save power.

  5. Call for monitoring

Monitoring: how we know it is dry

Each visit starts with the same set of readings so the numbers can be compared day to day. We record air temperature and relative humidity, then take moisture readings on marked points in walls, floors and framing. A daily log shows whether the numbers are falling, stalling or rising, and the equipment is adjusted to match.

The goal comes from the building itself. Following the ANSI/IICRC S500 reference for professional water damage restoration, we take readings from unaffected materials of the same type and use them as the drying goal. Equipment comes out when the wet materials reach that goal, not when the surface feels dry.

For indoor air, the EPA advises keeping relative humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent. During drying the air is often run lower than that on purpose, then returned to normal when the job ends.

What to expect while equipment runs

Drying equipment is loud. Air movers hum all day and night, and dehumidifiers put out heat, so rooms can feel warm. The units draw real power, so expect to see it on the next electric bill. We spread equipment across circuits to avoid tripping breakers.

Equipment keeps running after the surface feels dry because the surface dries first. Moisture deeper in the wall or floor is still moving out, and shutting down early lets it drift back toward the finish.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Turning the heat way up to dry walls faster, which can stress flooring and trim.
  • Turning equipment off at night because of noise, which resets progress.
  • Painting or re-flooring before the readings match the goal.
  • Leaving a sealed wall alone because the weather is dry outside.
