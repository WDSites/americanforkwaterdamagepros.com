Emergency Flood Damage Restoration in American Fork, UT

Runoff, groundwater, flooded basements and sewer backups, with safety first and 24/7 dispatch for the first call.

Call (385) 988-9410

Emergency flood damage restoration is the response to water that came from outside the plumbing: river rise, heavy runoff, groundwater or a sewer line that backed up. In American Fork the most common version is spring runoff in the foothills and along the American Fork River, plus the basement seepage that can follow.

Safety comes first. Floodwater and sewage can carry contamination, so stay out of standing water, keep children and pets away and call the utility or 911 before you call us if water reaches the panel or you smell gas. Dispatch is open 24/7 at (385) 988-9410, and we offer same-day response when available.

Spring runoff and American Fork's flood history

The city's river channel page says American Fork River runs through the city into Utah Lake and that spring runoff peaks from April through June 1. It adds that the city is not responsible for providing flood protection to personal property. In 2023, record snowpack led the state to declare an emergency over flooding and flood risks, and the city offered limited sandbags at 275 E 200 N.

The state's hazard mitigation plan lists Utah County in federal flood declarations in 1983, 1984 and 2011. That is a reminder that high-runoff years come back, so a plan made before the creek rises is worth more than one made during it.

Basement flooding cleanup

Basements flood from window wells, floor drains, groundwater pressure and failed sump pumps. A finished basement is the hardest case because carpet, drywall and insulation absorb the water and hold it against concrete. Crews pump the water out, remove materials that cannot be saved, dry the slab and walls and check moisture before anything is rebuilt.

Callers sometimes ask about waterproofing or a foundation inspection. Restoration is not waterproofing. If the basement floods again after the repair, the cause has to be fixed by someone who handles drainage and foundations, and we would rather say that up front.

Sewage backup: what to do first

Sewage is Category 3 water. The EPA, citing the S500 standard, calls it heavily contaminated and capable of carrying pathogens or toxins. Do not clean it yourself, do not run the drains and keep everyone away from the affected rooms. Porous materials that touched sewage, such as carpet, pad and drywall, are removed and discarded.

Report a backup to the city if it may come from the public line. American Fork's sewer and storm division is the contact for the city system, and the city's emergency page lists a sewer and storm water number. Then call us for removal, cleanup and sanitizing.

Insurance and flood coverage

The Utah Insurance Department says flood, earthquake, earth movement and mudslide are typically not covered on a homeowners policy, and a Utah Insurance Department flood FAQ states that standard flood policies usually have a 30-day waiting period. The state's hazard plan counted 695 active flood policies in Utah County as of January 2023. If your home sits near the channel, ask your insurer about flood coverage well before April.

After a loss, the department advises calling your insurer as soon as possible, taking steps to prevent further damage and waiting for the insurer to inspect before permanent repairs. We provide photos, readings and a written scope. We do not decide coverage.
