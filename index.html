First Hour

What To Do Before the Crew Arrives

Emergency water damage rewards fast, calm action. These steps are the same whether the water came from a supply line, an appliance or a storm, and they cost nothing to start.

If water is still spreading

  • ✓Stop the source. Close the fixture shutoff or the main water valve, and keep a pair of pliers near the valve if the handle is stiff.
  • ✓Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. If water touches outlets, panels or appliances, stay out and call the utility or 911 before you call us.
  • ✓Lift furniture legs onto blocks, pull up small rugs and move electronics and paper to dry rooms.
  • ✓Keep children and pets out of the water, especially when it came from a drain, a toilet or a sewer line.
  • ✓Photograph every wet surface and every damaged item before you move anything. Your insurer will ask for them.

Then call (385) 988-9410. Dispatch will ask where the water came from, how long it has been wet and what rooms are affected, because those answers decide which equipment goes on the truck.

Local Causes

Why American Fork Homes Get Wet

The city sits between American Fork Canyon and Utah Lake, and water reaches homes from more directions than most owners expect. Knowing the source helps because it decides whether the water is clean, how fast it spreads and what your policy is likely to say.

Spring runoff along the American Fork River

The city says the American Fork River runs through town and into Utah Lake, and that spring runoff peaks from April through June 1. In 2023, a record snowpack led the state to declare an emergency over flooding and flood risks, and the city offered limited sandbags at the Public Works lot at 275 E 200 N. If you live near the channel, check the FEMA flood map for your address, because a 2024 state notice about new Utah County flood maps lists American Fork among the cities where floodplain boundaries changed.

Irrigation lines and winter freezes

The city's pressurized irrigation system runs from about mid-April to mid-October, and the city advises homeowners to close the shutoff valve and possibly blow out sprinkler lines once it shuts down. A line that is left charged through a hard freeze can crack, and the break often stays hidden until spring. Water from that line is secondary water, which the state health department says is untreated, so tell the crew where it came from.

Aging supply lines and indoor plumbing

The city's water page notes that some of its own water lines are old and that crews replace some each year. Inside the home the same logic applies. Homes near the historic downtown, where the city's walking tour lists buildings from 1877 to 1911, include some of the oldest structures in town, so it is worth asking a plumber how old your supply lines are before one fails.

Our Process

From First Call to Restored

  1. Call dispatch

    Tell us what happened and what you see. We ask about the water source and how long it has been wet.

  2. Inspection and scope

    A crew checks moisture with meters and thermal imaging, then explains the scope and the price before work starts.

  3. Extract and dry

    Extraction units remove standing water, then air movers and dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the structure.

  4. Clean and rebuild

    Ruined materials come out, surfaces are cleaned and the structure is repaired to its pre-loss condition.

Drying is measured, not guessed. Moisture meters are checked against dry reference readings, and equipment stays until the wet materials read dry. Work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration where it applies, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Insurance Claims

Water Damage and Your Insurance

Coverage depends on your policy and on how the water got in. The Utah Insurance Department says flood, earthquake and mudslide are typically not covered on a homeowners policy, that backed-up drains and sewers are not covered under most standard policies, and that frozen pipes may not be covered if the damage is due to negligence.

Standard flood insurance usually carries a 30-day waiting period before coverage takes effect, so a policy bought after the snowpack warning arrives will not help with the runoff that follows. Ask your insurer which perils your policy names, and call your insurer as soon as it is safe to do so.

What to document for a claim

  • ✓Date and time you found the water, and where you believe it came from
  • ✓Photos and video of every wet room and every damaged item before cleanup
  • ✓Moisture readings and the written scope from the restoration crew
  • ✓Receipts for emergency repairs, since the department advises keeping them
  • ✓Your insurer's claim number and the adjuster's name

We document the loss and explain the scope in writing. We do not make coverage decisions, we do not promise approval and we do not bill insurers directly.
