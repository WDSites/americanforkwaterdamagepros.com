Emergency Water Heater Leak Cleanup in American Fork, UT

A tank water heater that leaks or fails can put a lot of water on a basement or garage floor before anyone notices. We remove that water and dry the structure, and a plumber handles the heater itself.

In many homes the water heater sits in a basement, a garage or a utility closet off the laundry room. That is convenient until the tank splits, a supply fitting drips or the relief valve starts discharging. The water spreads across a slab or soaks the bottom of finished walls, and a finished basement can hide it for hours.

How a water heater leak happens in American Fork homes

A tank heater can leak in four places: the tank body itself, the temperature and pressure relief valve, the hot and cold supply connections, and the drain valve near the bottom. A tank that rusts through is the serious one, because the whole tank can empty onto the floor.

According to the city's water quality report, American Fork's culinary water comes from two springs and five wells. Water from a failed heater is treated as clean at first, but sitting in carpet pad, drywall and framing it will not stay that way for long.

The US Department of Energy says most storage water heaters last 10 to 15 years, and it recommends draining a quart from the tank every three months to clear sediment. An older unit that has never been drained is a likely candidate for a tank leak.

First-hour safety steps

  1. Cut power or gas at the unit

    For an electric heater, turn off its breaker. For gas, turn the control to off or close the gas valve on the supply line. Do this only if you can reach it without standing in water.

  2. Close the cold-water inlet

    The shutoff valve is on the cold pipe entering the top of the tank. Turn the handle clockwise until it stops. If the valve is stuck, close the main water valve for the house instead.

  3. Stay away from outlets and cords

    If water has reached outlets, power strips or the electrical panel, do not walk through it or touch anything. Turn off the main breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.

  4. Be careful with hot water

    Water from the tank can scald, and a discharging relief valve may release very hot water. Keep children and pets back.

  5. Photograph, then call us

    Take photos and video of the unit, the water and the damage for your records, then call us so extraction can begin.

How we dry out a water heater loss

Because the source is clean supply water, this is usually Category 1 under ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard professional restorers work from. That gives materials a better chance of being saved if drying starts quickly. Look at emergency water removal for how the first visit works.

  • Extract standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction units.
  • Pull wet baseboard, and cut drywall a few inches above the wet line when it is soaked.
  • Take moisture meter readings and use a thermal camera to find wet areas behind finished walls.
  • Set air movers and dehumidifiers, and recheck readings until the structure reaches dry standards.

Insurance and water heater leaks

Policies differ, so ask your insurer how they treat a heater failure. In general terms, insurers distinguish a sudden event, such as a tank that bursts, from a slow leak that has gone on for weeks. Keep records either way.

The Utah Insurance Department advises calling your insurer as soon as possible, taking photos and holding off on permanent repairs until the company has inspected.

Mistakes to avoid and strapping

  • Letting the heater keep running while you decide what to do.
  • Using a household vacuum on standing water. It is not built for it and it risks shock.
  • Walking through water near the panel or outlets.
  • Assuming a dry-looking wall is dry. Moisture hides behind finished basement walls.

Utah's plumbing code amendments say water heaters must be anchored or strapped against horizontal movement, and the Utah earthquake program recommends strapping as well. The code was amended recently, so ask a plumber to confirm the current rules. Heaters near Pleasant Grove or Lehi follow the same state code.
