When standing water is an emergency

Water that you can see across a floor is already soaking into carpet pad, baseboards and the bottom of walls. The longer it stands, the higher it wicks. Standing water on a finished basement floor, water coming through a ceiling, and water pooling around a water heater or washing machine all call for immediate removal.

Burst pipe water damage

A burst pipe is the classic after-hours call, and the first move is always the same: close the main shutoff so the water stops. Extraction starts once the source is off. The steps for a line that failed under pressure, and how wet walls and ceilings below the break are handled, are on the burst pipe cleanup page. A pipe that split in the cold has its own page on frozen pipe water damage.

What a crew does on arrival

The crew confirms the source is stopped, checks for electrical hazards and starts with extraction. Truck-mounted or portable units pull water from carpet and hard floors, submersible pumps handle deeper water and weighted extractors work on carpet that is saturated. The crew then takes moisture readings so the drying plan matches what the water actually did.

What American Fork residents should know about the water lines

The city supplies culinary water from springs in American Fork Canyon and five deep wells, and its water page notes that some city lines are old and that the city replaces some each year. Water laterals are required to be buried at least 48 inches deep unless the city engineer approves otherwise, according to the city's standards drawings. A break on the street side belongs to the city, and the city's after-hours water number is 801-404-1253.

A break on your side of the meter is yours to stop and fix. If you cannot tell which side the leak is on, shut off your own valve first, then call the city, then call us.
