Emergency Storm Water Damage in American Fork, UT

After a hard storm, water reaches a house from several directions at once. We extract it, dry the structure and clear wet debris. Board-up and exterior repairs belong to other trades.

Call (385) 988-9410

A storm event rarely floods a home in one neat way. Wind drives rain around window frames, hail dents roofing, limbs land on gutters, window wells fill and runoff finds the lowest door. In American Fork the damage often shows up the next morning, when someone walks into a wet basement or notices a stain.

How storms get water into American Fork homes

The Utah State Hazard Mitigation Plan, written by the state's emergency management division, says thunderstorm straight-line winds can exceed 100 mph. It names Utah County among the Wasatch Front counties exposed to severe weather. The same plan defines a cloudburst as rain at 3.9 inches per hour or more.

The city describes a public storm drain system that carries runoff from rain, snow and hail to the storm drain and on toward Utah Lake. It is the public system and not a promise about any one lot. The city's technical manual designs storm drain pipes for the 25-year storm, so a larger event can outrun them.

  • Wind-driven rain pushing past siding, window frames and door thresholds.
  • Hail or fallen limbs damaging roofing and gutters.
  • Clogged gutters and downspouts spilling beside the foundation.
  • Window wells filling faster than they drain.
  • Surface runoff reaching a basement door or a low wall.
  • A power outage that stops a sump pump.

First-hour safety steps

  1. Stay away from downed lines

    Treat any fallen power line as live. Keep people and pets back and call the utility or emergency services.

  2. Be careful with electricity

    Do not use outlets or touch the panel while standing in water. If water is near the panel or the floor is wet, stay out and call an electrician.

  3. Check the sump pump

    If the power is out, a sump pump will not run. Do not carry a generator indoors, and do not wade into a flooded basement.

  4. Do not drive through water

    Moving water on a road can carry a vehicle away, and the depth is hard to judge.

  5. Photograph before cleanup

    Take photos and video of the water, the damage and any broken windows or roofing before you move things.

How we extract, dry and clear debris

We use ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard for professional water damage restoration, as our reference. Storm water may carry mud, so we treat it as contaminated until a professional says otherwise.

  • Extraction units remove standing water from floors and carpet.
  • Wet carpet pad, soaked drywall and insulation are removed where they cannot be dried.
  • Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the room and the wall cavities.
  • Moisture meters and a thermal camera track where water traveled.
  • Wet debris and mud are bagged and removed from the affected rooms.

Board-up, window replacement, gutter repair and roofing are not our work. We dry and clean what is wet. General pump-out work is also described on emergency water removal, and we serve Lehi and Pleasant Grove with the same process.

Where insurance and the city fit

The city says it is not responsible for providing flood protection to personal property. The Utah Insurance Department says interior water damage from a storm, when there is no damage to the roof or walls, is often not covered, and flood is typically excluded from a homeowners policy. Policies differ, so ask your insurer, keep every receipt and photo and do not wait for an adjuster to start drying.
