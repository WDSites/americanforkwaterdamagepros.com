Commercial Water Damage in American Fork, UT

Water in a commercial space raises questions a house does not: who is responsible, what the lease says, and who is on the other side of the wall. This page covers the water work we do and the people you will need to coordinate with.

Call (385) 988-9410

Water reaches an office, shop, storage unit or other commercial space the same ways it reaches a home: a failed supply line, a leaking roof, a drain backup or a discharged fire sprinkler. The difference is the setting.

Walls and ceilings are often shared, equipment sits on the floor, and several parties have a say in what happens next. American Fork had 33,337 residents at the 2020 Census. Wherever commercial space shares walls, floors or ceilings, one wet suite can affect its neighbors.

Water sources in a commercial building

Supply lines, drains, rooftop units and roof leaks are the usual starting points. Sprinkler discharge deserves its own mention because it releases a lot of water quickly. The Utah State Fire Marshal rule R710-5 requires sprinkler systems to be inspected annually by registered persons, and it calls for a red tag when a system is impaired. Only the local fire authority can remove that tag.

American Fork Fire Prevention inspects businesses annually, according to the city. If a sprinkler head goes off, call 911 first when there is a fire, then deal with the water.

What you see and what it means

  • Stained ceiling tiles: water is moving above the ceiling and may have soaked insulation.
  • Bubbling paint or peeling wallpaper on a shared wall: moisture is passing into the neighboring suite.
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot: the pad and slab are holding water.
  • A musty smell in a closed storage room: damp materials have been sitting.

Who decides and who pays

Responsibility depends on the building. The owner, the tenant and a property manager may each control a different part of the space. Read your lease to see who handles repairs and who carries building insurance, then call the insurer. We cannot give legal advice, and we do not settle disputes between owner and tenant.

What we can do is talk with the building manager or owner and the insurer's adjuster about the water work. We share what we find so the people who decide have the same information.

Safety in the first hour

First steps in a wet commercial space

  • ✓Find the water shutoff for the suite or building and close it if a supply line is the source.
  • ✓Stay away from outlets, power strips and floor-level equipment, and shut off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry.
  • ✓Block off slippery floors so staff and visitors do not walk through.
  • ✓Move records and electronics to a dry spot if it is safe to do so.
  • ✓Call the building manager, then call (385) 988-9410.

If the water came from outside or from a drain, treat it as unsafe and read about flood damage restoration or sewage backup cleanup. Keeping people out of wet areas matters more than keeping the doors open.

Stay safe while you wait

  • ✓Keep people out of rooms where water touches outlets, panels or equipment
  • ✓Do not walk on ceiling tiles or under sagging ceilings
  • ✓Move records and electronics to a dry area only if it is safe to do so
  • ✓Take photos of the damage and note the time you found it

How we handle the water work

  1. Assess and scope

    We trace where the water went, map wet areas with moisture meters and a thermal camera, and record the findings.

  2. Extract

    Pumps and extraction units remove standing water from floors and low spots.

  3. Remove wet finishes

    Soaked carpet, ceiling tiles, baseboards and drywall come out when they cannot dry in place.

  4. Dry and clean

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and we clean the surfaces we remove.

  5. Document

    Photos, moisture logs and notes go to the building manager and the adjuster.

The drying plan follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the reference standard for professional water damage restoration. Our water mitigation page explains the same work in a residential setting.

Repairs, permits and what we do not do

We do not do plumbing repair, roofing or electrical repair. Those belong to the right trade. The city says a permit is required to repair or replace plumbing, mechanical, gas or electrical systems, so a contractor should check with the Building Department before work starts.

Business interruption is an insurance question. Ask your insurer what your policy provides for lost income or a temporary closure, and keep records.
