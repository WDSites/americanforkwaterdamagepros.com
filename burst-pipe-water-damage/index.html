Emergency Burst Pipe Cleanup in American Fork, UT

A burst supply line can put a large volume of water into a house in a short time, in any season. We extract that water and dry the structure, and a plumber repairs the pipe.

Pressurized water behaves differently from a slow drip. When a fitting lets go or a braided connector splits, water keeps coming until someone closes a valve. In American Fork that can happen in a newer subdivision with plastic lines or in an older house with copper pipe.

This page is about supply lines that fail at any time of year, and the cold-weather cause has its own page on frozen pipes.

How a pressurized line fails inside a house

The usual failure points are compression fittings, solder joints, washing machine and toilet connectors, ice maker lines and the short flexible lines under sinks. A pinhole in a copper run can spray into a wall cavity for days before a stain appears.

Water also travels. It follows framing, wiring holes and plumbing penetrations down through a floor and into the ceiling of the room below, and in a basement it soaks the bottom of finished walls. The city supplies culinary water from springs in American Fork Canyon and from deep wells, so the water starts clean, but it does not stay that way once it sits in carpet pad and drywall.

A pipe inside a wall can leak unseen for a long time, so a dry-looking surface is not proof that the cavity is dry. We check with a moisture meter and a thermal camera before deciding what to open.

First-hour safety steps

  1. Close the main valve

    Look where the line enters the house, often a basement wall, a utility room, a garage or the laundry area. Turn a round handle clockwise until it stops. A lever handle is closed when it sits crosswise to the pipe.

  2. Open the lowest tap

    With the main closed, open a basement sink or an outdoor spigot to drain pressure from the pipes so the run slows and stops.

  3. Mind the electricity

    If water reached outlets, light fixtures or the panel, leave the breaker alone unless you can reach it from dry floor. Never stand in water to touch electrical equipment, and stay out from under a ceiling that sags or bulges.

  4. Check the street side

    The meter box sits in the park strip and the city treats it as its own equipment. The city's standard drawing calls for a box 21 by 36 inches. If your valve will not turn or the break looks like it is on the street side, the city's water department lists an after-hours line on its water page.

  5. Make the two calls

    A plumber repairs the pipe. We handle extraction and drying, so call us while you wait for the plumber.

  6. Photograph before moving things

    Take photos or video of the wet areas and the failed part before cleanup, and keep the part if the plumber removes it.

How we remove the water and dry the structure

We work from ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard for professional water damage restoration, as our reference. This is different from the general pump-out work described on emergency water removal, because a supply line failure often leaves water inside walls and under floors, not only on top of them.

  • Extraction units pull standing water and water out of carpet and pad.
  • Flood cuts open a wet wall along clean, straight lines just above the visible wet line, which keeps the later patch simple.
  • Wet insulation and soaked drywall come out, since they hold water against framing.
  • Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the cavity and the room together.
  • Moisture meter and thermal camera readings go on a moisture map, and we repeat the readings until framing is dry.

The EPA says to dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, so the first day matters. More on drying is under structural drying. The same steps apply to homes in Pleasant Grove and Lehi, which we also serve.

Where insurance fits

Policies differ on sudden failures compared with long-term leaks, and on damage the insurer decides came from neglect. The Utah Insurance Department tells policyholders to call their insurer as soon as possible, photograph the damage and hold off on permanent repairs until the property has been inspected. Ask your insurer what applies to your policy and keep every receipt and photo.
