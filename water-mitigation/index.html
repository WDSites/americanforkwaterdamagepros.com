Emergency water mitigation is the first response to water in a building. A crew stops the loss, removes standing water and sets up drying equipment so moisture does not spread into walls, floors and framing. Dispatch is open 24/7 because the first day decides how much has to be torn out.

If water is still moving, stop the source first. Close the fixture shutoff or the main valve, turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel safely and call the utility or 911 before you call us if water touches electrical equipment. Then call (385) 988-9410 and keep moving belongings while the crew is arranged.

When standing water becomes an emergency

Water is an emergency the moment it reaches anything porous or electrical. Carpet, drywall and insulation wick moisture upward and outward, and the EPA says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. After that window the job shifts from drying to replacing.

Wet carpet, damp walls and a musty smell: that is a mitigation job

Most people do not search for the trade name. They notice carpet that squishes, a wall that feels cool and damp or a smell that appears a day after a leak. Each of those means moisture is inside the structure, and drying it is exactly what mitigation does. If you can describe it, we can tell you whether it needs a crew.

  1. Shut off and assess

    The source is stopped and the crew maps where the water traveled using moisture meters and a thermal camera.

  2. Remove standing water

    Extraction units and pumps pull out what is on the floor and in low spots.

  3. Set drying equipment

    Air movers and low-grain dehumidifiers go where the readings call for them, not simply into the middle of the room.

  4. Monitor daily

    Readings are taken each visit and equipment is adjusted until the wet materials read dry.

Structural drying: what the equipment does

Structural drying is the part of mitigation that happens after the standing water is gone. Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces, dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air so it cannot settle back, and moisture meters show whether the drying is working. Wet insulation and carpet pad are usually removed because they hold water far longer than the room around them.

What American Fork homes add to the picture

The city's pressurized irrigation system is available from about mid-April to mid-October, and the city advises closing the shutoff valve and possibly blowing out sprinkler lines after the shutdown. A line left charged through a freeze can crack and leak into a basement or crawl space long before anyone notices. Because secondary water is untreated, tell the crew where the water came from so wet materials are handled correctly.

Basements finished by earlier owners deserve special care. Drywall and carpet that sit directly against a concrete wall can hide moisture for weeks, and a moisture reading is the only way to know whether a wall is truly dry.

What decides the cost

Mitigation is priced on the equipment, the days it stays and the labor to set it up and monitor it. The crew explains the scope and price before work starts, and the factors below are the ones that change it.

Cost factors for mitigation

  • ✓The water category, since contaminated water adds removal and sanitizing
  • ✓The area and the number of rooms affected
  • ✓The materials that got wet and whether they can dry in place
  • ✓The days of equipment on site, driven by how slowly the materials dry
  • ✓Whether rebuilding is needed afterward, which is scoped separately

Mitigation can fall under the sudden and accidental water coverage in many homeowners policies, and often does not when the cause was a long-running leak. Ask your insurer, and keep your photos, readings and receipts, since the Utah Insurance Department advises keeping receipts for emergency repairs.

Where mitigation fits in the larger job

After drying, water damage cleanup removes what could not be saved, and water damage repair rebuilds. If the water came from a river rise or a storm, the flood damage restoration page covers the extra steps. Crews work the same way in Lindon and the neighboring towns.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration, and the EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Ask any company whether its crews work to that standard, and call early, because the drying window is short.
