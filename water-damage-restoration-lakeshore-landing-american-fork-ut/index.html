Water Damage Restoration in Lakeshore Landing, American Fork, UT

Compact lots, shared walls and homeowner associations change how a leak spreads and who needs a phone call. Here is what to do first in a Lakeshore Landing home and how drying works in attached buildings.

Lakeshore Landing sits on the west side of American Fork, roughly between 363 and 496 South along 890 to 920 West according to city planning notices. The city places part of it in a Transit Oriented Development zone, and a separate site plan covers amenities for the community's homeowners association. A listing site shows townhomes built in 2022 on 900 West, and a subdivision summary reports that nearly all sampled homes in one section were built in 2020 or later.

We are not describing every building here, because styles within the community differ. The point is that newer, compact, attached homes behave differently from a detached house on a large lot.

Why shared walls change the plan

  • Water can pass through a shared wall, floor or ceiling into a neighbor's unit, so one leak can become two claims.
  • Stacked units mean a leak upstairs reaches the ceiling below within minutes.
  • Small footprints leave little room to stage equipment, so access and parking plans matter.
  • The association may own parts of the building envelope, shared plumbing or the roof.

Call your association or property manager early. They can tell you who owns which pipe and whether other units are affected. Our structural drying page explains how we dry walls, floors and cavities without tearing out more than needed.

Safety in the first hour

What to do before anyone arrives

  • ✓Shut the water at the fixture valve, or at the main if you cannot find the fixture valve.
  • ✓Turn off power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and reachable. Never stand in water to reach a breaker.
  • ✓Warn neighbors below and beside you if water is moving through the building.
  • ✓Photograph damage and the source before cleanup starts.
  • ✓Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.

Common sources in newer attached homes

Newer materials do not prevent leaks. Water heaters, supply lines, dishwashers and air conditioning drain lines still fail. Our guides to water heater leak cleanup, appliance leak water damage and AC leak water damage cover each case.

Because we do not repair plumbing or appliances, the usual sequence is that a plumber or technician stops the source, and we handle the water and drying.

How drying is planned in attached buildings

  1. Check beyond your unit

    Meters are used along shared walls and floors to see whether neighboring spaces are wet.

  2. Remove standing water

    Extraction comes first so drying equipment is not fighting a flooded floor.

  3. Dry cavities and floors

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings match dry reference points, guided by the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

  4. Coordinate with the association

    Records of readings and photos help when several parties are involved.
