Does Condo Insurance Cover Water Damage in American Fork, UT?

How the association's policy and your own policy divide a leak, what happens when the unit above floods yours, and what to ask before you file.

Call (385) 988-9410

Condo insurance may cover water damage, but which policy pays depends on where the water started and which parts of the building each policy covers. In most condos and townhomes, the association's policy covers the building's shared structure and your own policy covers the parts you are responsible for, such as interior finishes and your belongings. A sudden, accidental leak is often covered. A slow leak that went unnoticed often is not.

Because every association writes its own rules, there is no single answer. This guide explains how the two layers fit together, what to do in the first hours and which questions to put to the association and your insurer.

Two policies, two layers of responsibility

The Utah Insurance Department tells condo and townhome owners to know the coverage they need for the part of the building they are responsible for. It also says you are responsible for understanding how your own policy fits with the association's policy and its governing documents, often called the CC&Rs.

Those documents decide where your responsibility starts. In one building, the association may insure everything from the drywall out. In another, you may be responsible for walls, flooring, cabinets and fixtures inside the unit. Read the section on insurance and maintenance before you ever have a leak.

The deductible trap on small losses

The Utah Insurance Department warns that the association's policy deductible might be higher than your loss, so a small loss may not be paid by the association's policy. A wet carpet and a stained ceiling can fall well under that line. When it does, the repair bill moves to whoever the documents or the facts of the leak point to.

That is why a quick call to the association manager matters on day one. Ask what the master policy deductible is and how the association has handled small losses before. The answer tells you whether to plan on using your own coverage from the start.

What to do in the first hours

  1. Stop the water if you can

    Close the shutoff valve for the fixture or the unit. If you cannot find it, call the association manager or maintenance line.

  2. Call your insurer or agent

    The Utah Insurance Department advises calling as soon as possible, so start the claim before the cleanup is finished.

  3. Notify the association in writing

    Send an email or letter so there is a dated record, and keep a copy.

  4. Photograph and video everything

    Capture the source, the wet areas, damaged belongings and any stains on ceilings or walls before anything is moved.

  5. Dry the unit and keep receipts

    The department advises taking temporary steps to prevent further damage and keeping receipts. Hold off on permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected.

Why drying cannot wait for the claim

The EPA says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and to discard what has been wet longer and cannot be dried. A claim decision can take longer than that, so waiting for approval can turn a dry-out into a mold problem.

In an attached building, water also travels. It can run down through a wall cavity into the unit below, or sideways to a shared wall. Our water damage cleanup crews remove standing water, set drying equipment and document readings, which gives your insurer a record to work from. Questions for the association and your insurer

Ask the association: what does the master policy cover inside units, what is its deductible and who maintains the pipe, roof or riser that failed. Ask for the answers in writing and ask how the association wants losses reported.

Ask your insurer: does the policy treat this as sudden and accidental, what is the deductible, what does it cover for loss of use if you must move out and what documentation does it want. In general terms, sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not and flood is usually excluded, but your policy governs. Ask your insurer before you assume.

What condo and townhome owners in American Fork should keep in mind

The Utah Insurance Department says that if you cannot resolve a problem with your insurer, you can file a complaint online, and the process usually takes three to four weeks. The department cannot settle disputes like a court, so build your file early with dated notes and photos rather than waiting for a dispute. Its phone number is (801) 957-9305.

The department's claim steps apply to every unit: call early, protect the property and keep receipts. Owners in nearby Lindon and Pleasant Grove face the same sorting of association and personal coverage, so the questions in this guide carry over.
