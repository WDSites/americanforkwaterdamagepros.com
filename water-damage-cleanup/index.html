Emergency Water Damage Cleanup in American Fork, UT

Removing wet debris, ruined materials and contamination after the water is stopped, then sanitizing and deodorizing what stays.

Call (385) 988-9410

Emergency water damage cleanup begins once the water source is stopped. A crew removes wet debris, pulls out materials that cannot be saved, washes and sanitizes what stays and prepares the structure for drying. In American Fork this is most often called for after a supply-line failure, an appliance leak or a backup.

The first 48 hours are the part to take seriously. The EPA recommends discarding anything wet for more than 24 to 48 hours that cannot be dried, and cleanup that starts that day has the best chance of saving flooring and furniture. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, and a crew can often be sent the same day when one is available.

Clean, gray or contaminated: what the water category means

How the water got into your home decides what cleanup involves. Industry practice under ANSI/IICRC S500 sorts water into three categories, and the EPA, citing that standard, describes them in the table below. The category can worsen as time passes, which is another reason not to wait.

Water categories and what they mean for cleanup
CategorySourceCleanup approach
Category 1A sanitary source such as a supply lineExtract, dry and clean. Most materials survive if handled quickly.
Category 2Significantly contaminated, such as a washing machine drainRemove porous materials, clean and sanitize hard surfaces.
Category 3Heavily contaminated, such as sewage or floodwaterStay out, call a professional, remove and discard exposed porous materials.

What cleanup includes

  1. Remove debris and wet contents

    Ruined items and loose debris go out first so crews can reach the surfaces.

  2. Cut out unsalvageable material

    Soaked carpet pad, insulation and drywall come out along a clean line.

  3. Wash and sanitize

    Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned with detergent and disinfected where the category calls for it.

  4. Deodorize and verify

    Odor sources are removed and moisture readings confirm the area is ready to dry.

A local detail that changes the category

American Fork runs a pressurized irrigation system for outdoor use, and the Utah Department of Health and Human Services says secondary water is not treated and may contain harmful germs. If a broken irrigation line wet your basement, do not treat it as clean water. Tell the crew so the wet materials are removed and sanitized correctly.

Safety before cleanup

Do not wade through water that touches outlets, appliances or the electrical panel. Cut power only if you can reach the panel without standing in water, and call the utility or 911 before calling us if you smell gas or see sparks. For sewage or floodwater, the CDC says the best protection is to stay out of the water.

Documenting the loss

Insurers ask what was wet and what was removed, so cleanup is documented as it happens. The crew photographs affected areas, notes the materials removed and records moisture readings. The Utah Insurance Department advises keeping receipts for emergency repairs and waiting for the insurer's inspection before permanent repairs, which is why cleanup and repair are kept as separate steps.
