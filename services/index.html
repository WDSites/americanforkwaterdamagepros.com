Water Damage and Mold Services in American Fork, UT

Six services that cover a water loss from the first call to the finished repair, with scope and price explained before work starts.

Call (385) 988-9410

A water loss moves through stages, and each stage has its own work. The pages below follow that order, from stopping the water to rebuilding the room. If you are not sure where your situation sits, call (385) 988-9410 and dispatch will help you sort it out.

The first day: stopping and removing

Emergency water removal is the answer to water standing on a floor, in a basement or in a crawl space, and emergency water mitigation is the drying that has to follow in the first 24 to 72 hours. Both are dispatched around the clock.

The first week: clearing what cannot be saved

Water damage cleanup removes soaked debris and materials that cannot be dried, and it is also where sanitizing happens when the water was contaminated. When the source was a river rise, a storm or a sewer line, flood damage restoration adds the safety steps those causes demand.

After drying: rebuilding

Water damage repair covers drywall, ceilings, flooring, insulation and paint. It is scoped separately so you can see the price of rebuilding apart from the price of the emergency work.

When moisture lingers: mold

Mold remediation is the secondary service. It follows water damage that stayed wet, and it starts with the moisture source because mold needs water to grow.

What is not on this list

We do not handle fire and smoke damage, waterproofing or foundation work, roofing or plumbing repair. When your problem needs one of those trades, we say so and point you in that direction.

Where each service is available

Every service above is dispatched from American Fork, ZIP 84003, to the neighboring towns of Pleasant Grove, Lehi, Lindon and Alpine. The service area page shows the map.

How pricing works

We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and we do not post exact figures online. Water category, affected area, materials, drying days and rebuild scope decide the number, and each service page lists the factors that matter for that work.

How the services connect on a typical job

A common sequence after a burst pipe
StageServiceWhat happens
Hour oneEmergency water removalStanding water is pumped and extracted
Day one to threeWater mitigationFans and dehumidifiers dry the structure while meters track progress
Day two to fiveWater damage cleanupSoaked materials that cannot be dried are removed and surfaces are cleaned
After readings are dryWater damage repairDrywall, insulation, flooring and paint are replaced
If moisture lingeredMold remediationThe source is fixed, then affected materials are cleaned or removed

Not every job uses every stage. A small appliance leak caught within hours may need only mitigation and a patch, while a basement flood can use all five. The crew writes the scope for your house and explains it before work starts.

Why the order matters

The EPA recommends drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, so the early stages are where time counts. Repair comes last because covering a wet wall traps moisture. Each service page explains its stage in detail and links to the next one.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration and the S520 standard for mold remediation are the published benchmarks for this work. You can ask any company whether its crews follow them.
