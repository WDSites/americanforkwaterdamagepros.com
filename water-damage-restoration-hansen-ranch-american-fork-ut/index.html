Water Damage Restoration in Hansen Ranch, American Fork, UT

Larger homes with big garages and unfinished space hold more water and take longer to dry. Here is what to do first in a Hansen Ranch home in east American Fork and what a drying job involves.

Call (385) 988-9410

Hansen Ranch is a community on the east side of American Fork. City planning minutes list a Hansen Ranch phase at 1100 East 400 North. A 2019 Daily Herald article about the community says model homes stood at that address and that the builder planned 90 homes. The article describes seven floor plans between 2,000 and 3,300 finished square feet, and every plan has a three-car garage.

We are not claiming anything about specific houses. The point is that bigger floor areas and attached garages change how a water problem spreads.

What bigger homes mean for water damage

  • More floor area means more material to extract, dry and monitor.
  • A three-car garage often holds a water heater, laundry or water lines that can leak or freeze.
  • Unfinished space, which the article counts in its larger square-foot figure, tends to hold storage that gets wet along with the structure.
  • A two-story plan lets water travel from an upstairs fixture to a main-floor ceiling.

Our frozen pipe water damage page is relevant for garage and exterior walls. The roof leak water damage page covers water coming from above.

Freeze days and snowmelt

NOAA climate normals for the nearest stations, in Pleasant Grove and Provo, show about 123 freeze days a year and a January average low near 24 degrees Fahrenheit. Pipes in garages, exterior walls and hose bibs are the usual freeze points. The city also reports that American Fork River flows peak between April and June 1, so spring is a second period of risk for water.

Safety in the first hour

Do these things in order

  • ✓Shut the main water valve. In larger homes it may be in a garage, utility room or basement.
  • ✓Keep clear of standing water near outlets or appliances. Do not touch the panel unless you are on dry ground.
  • ✓If the ceiling is sagging, keep people out of that room.
  • ✓Move vehicles, tools and boxes out of a wet garage if it is safe to do so.
  • ✓Photograph the source and damage.

How we handle a large-area job

  1. Map the water

    Meters and a thermal camera show how far water traveled across floors, walls and ceilings.

  2. Extract

    Standing water is extracted. Pad and insulation that will not dry in place are cut out.

  3. Dry

    More air movers and dehumidifiers are staged as needed, following ANSI/IICRC S500.

  4. Monitor

    Readings are logged until materials reach dry targets.
